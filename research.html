ABSTRACT

Most of an AI agent's model calls don't write anything; they decide. Is this action safe? Which team should handle this? Does a person need to see it? Today each of these is a full LLM call. Decido is a family of models built for these judgment calls: given a document and typed questions (yes/no, pick one, or a score), it returns a calibrated probability for every option, read directly from the model instead of generated as text. Decido-1, a 26B mixture-of-experts model (4B active parameters), runs CPU-only on a laptop, gives bit-identical answers on every call, and scores 85.4 on the six-benchmark suite used to evaluate TypeSafe's Jev 1.13 (83.9). With reasoning, Decido-1-Think scores 88.7 and Decido-1-Max, which distils our 3-model research system (89.57, not released) into one set of weights, scores 89.2; both are ahead of Jev on all six benchmarks. All models are trained on public data, with no outputs from Jev or any closed model, and audited for benchmark leakage.

3Method

  • Readout, not generation. The document and each question are placed in a fixed template; every option maps to a single label token. The answer distribution is the model's next-token probability over those labels. Nothing is generated or parsed.
  • Training. Gemma 4 26B-A4B with LoRA adapters on attention, the dense MLP, the router and all 128 experts. Soft targets mix gold labels (0.7) with a temperature-calibrated open teacher, Qwen3.8-27B (0.3, Apache-2.0); the loss combines soft cross-entropy, Brier score and ranked probability score.
  • Calibration. Temperatures per question type, fitted on held-out tasks (tasks not seen in training), which transfer better than temperatures fitted on seen tasks.
  • Deterministic runtime. An 8-bit GGUF file on llama.cpp with a fixed-size chunked prefill, so outputs are bit-identical regardless of thread timing or previous requests.
  • Decido-1-Fast. Qwen3-1.7B fully fine-tuned on one million questions labelled 0.5 gold + 0.5 Decido-1.
  • Decido-1-Think (released). Decido-1 with reasoning (greedy, up to 1,024 tokens), trained with hybrid STaR: on its own rationales, including wrong ones, with gold-anchored answers. 88.7 on GPU; on a laptop CPU it agrees with the GPU run on 59 of 60 sampled questions at ~19 s per question.
  • Decido-1-Ultra (released). Qwen3-0.6B, trained like Decido-1-Fast: 62.2, 0.41 s for five questions on a laptop CPU.
  • Research system (3 models, not released). A per-question gated mixture of Decido-1, Decido-1-Think and Qwen3.8-27B with reasoning, zero-shot: 89.57 on GPU. The configuration was fixed by a pre-registered rule on development data before any benchmark run.
  • Decido-1-Max (released). The research system distilled into one model: starting from Decido-1-Think, the answer step was trained on its own greedy reasoning for 100k questions with 0.5 gold-anchored + 0.5 research-system targets; the checkpoint was chosen by a pre-registered dev rule. 89.2 on GPU, statistically indistinguishable from the research system (−0.34 points, 95% CI −0.97 to +0.29). On a Mac it chose the same answer as the GPU run on 101 of 101 sampled questions (~11 s per question on the Mac GPU).
  • Data hygiene. Public datasets only; no Jev or closed-model outputs; no test splits; every training set gated for exact, n-gram and structural overlap with the benchmarks. Exceptions are disclosed on the model cards (Decido-1-XL's data includes 8 gate-passing rows derived from eval splits).

4Limitations

  • Decido-1 is trained on the public train splits of the benchmarks' task families. Without them it scores 82.0, below Jev. Every Decido model shares this dependence: Decido-1-Think and Decido-1-Max build on Decido-1, Decido-1-Fast and Decido-1-Ultra are distilled from it, and Decido-1-XL was trained with in-domain rows (no ablation was run for it); the research system's zero-shot Qwen component alone, with no task-specific training, scores 85.8.
  • Jev's numbers are its published figures; we have not run Jev on our harness. All Decido OpenJev scores cover 18 of its 19 tasks (one task's candidate pool is unpublished); Jev's 72.96 is a 19-task mean. Our 1.5-point margin on CPU is close to Jev's reported run-to-run spread (up to 1.6).
  • Decido-1-Max and Decido-1-Think write out their reasoning before answering, so they are slow on a laptop (~10–28 s per question) and not bit-identical across batches on a GPU server. On JevBench the research system's zero-shot Qwen component alone scores 99.3, and Decido-1-Max (96.1) is partly trained on the research system's answers; exposure of the public items during Qwen's pretraining can't be ruled out.
  • Calibration is measured on benchmark tasks. It is not yet measured on agent traces such as tool calls or shell commands; validate thresholds on your own data before automating.
