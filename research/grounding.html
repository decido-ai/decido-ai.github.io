ABSTRACT

An AI agent that answers from documents, such as a RAG assistant, a support agent or a research agent, needs to know whether what it is about to say is actually backed by its sources. LLM-AggreFact is the standard public test of that check: given a document and a claim, is the claim fully supported? It pools 11 human-labelled datasets, from news summaries and meeting notes to RAG answers and expert long-form answers. Decido-1 Grounding, our grounding checker, scores 79.2 mean balanced accuracy on the test split, second on the leaderboard after nimble-v4-max (79.5) and ahead of Clef (79.0), Decider v1.2 (78.7) and Jev 1.13 (78.7). The released, open Decido-1, a 4B-active model that runs on a laptop and was not trained further for this benchmark, scores 78.5, ahead of Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B (77.4) and Claude-3.5 Sonnet (77.2).

2How we tested

  • Data. The LLM-AggreFact test split, all 29,320 claims (lytang/LLM-AggreFact). Metric: balanced accuracy per dataset, then the unweighted mean of the 11, as on the leaderboard. A failed request would count as "not supported"; there were none.
  • No tuning on test. Every choice (prompt, models, decision threshold) was made on the dev split and frozen before the system saw test. One global threshold for all datasets, since a real checker doesn't know where a claim came from: 0.55 for Decido-1 Grounding, 0.5 for Decido-1. Each configuration was frozen on dev before it was scored on test; four were scored in total (77.9 to 79.3). We report this one because it uses only our own models.
  • No test data in training. Nothing from LLM-AggreFact dev or test, or from the test splits it was drawn from, was used to train Decido-1 Grounding. Every grounding training example was checked for exact, normalised and near-duplicate overlap with dev and test. No outputs of GPT, Claude or Jev were used.
  • Decido-1 Grounding is a proprietary system built only from models we trained. It runs on a GPU. We don't publish its recipe.

3Try it

The open Decido-1 checks a claim in one call (pip install decido-ai; weights on Hugging Face). This is the exact question we used for the 78.5 result:

from decido import Decido
m = Decido("decido-ai/decido-1-26b-a4b")
q = ("Is the following claim fully supported by the document? Answer yes only if every part of the "
     "claim is stated in the document or follows directly from it. Judge only from the document, "
     "not from outside knowledge.\n\nClaim: " + claim)
out = m.predict({"state": {"document": document}, "questions": {"supported": {"type": "noul", "instructions": q}}})
p = out["answers"]["supported"]["noul"]   # P(supported); "supported" if p >= 0.5

Decido-1 Grounding is not public yet; write to us to try it on your own data.

4Limitations

  • The margin over the next entries is small: 79.2 vs 79.0 for Clef and 78.7 for Jev 1.13 is inside our 95% interval (78.2–80.2). We are #2, not #1: nimble-v4-max scores 79.5.
  • Leaderboard numbers for other systems are taken from the public leaderboard, not rerun by us. Our numbers are not yet listed there.
  • Decido-1 was scored on a GPU (bf16). The laptop build gives the same answer on 99.6% of 231 checked claims and accepts documents up to 32k tokens; a few test documents are longer. Decido-1's own training data, built before this work and also the starting point of Decido-1 Grounding, was not separately audited against LLM-AggreFact.
  • ExpertQA stays near 60 for every system, which suggests label noise or claims that need outside knowledge.
