ABSTRACT

Most of an AI agent's model calls don't write anything; they decide. Is this action safe? Which team should handle this? Does a person need to see it? Today each of these is a full LLM call. Decido is a family of models built for these judgment calls: given a document and typed questions (yes/no, pick one, or a score), it returns a calibrated probability for every option, read directly from the model instead of generated as text. Decido-1, a 26B mixture-of-experts model (4B active parameters), runs CPU-only on a laptop, gives bit-identical answers on every call, and scores 85.4 on the six-benchmark suite used to evaluate TypeSafe's Jev 1.13 (83.9). With reasoning, Decido-1-Think scores 88.7 and Decido-1-Max scores 89.2; both are ahead of Jev on all six benchmarks. No outputs from Jev or any closed model were used, and every model is audited for benchmark leakage.

The agent blocks the request because injection is above its threshold, and doesn't act on urgency, where the model is unsure. A calibrated probability lets the agent act when the model is sure and ask a person when it isn't.

3Limitations

  • Jev's numbers are its published figures; we have not run Jev on our harness. All Decido OpenJev scores cover 18 of its 19 tasks (one task's candidate pool is unpublished); Jev's 72.96 is a 19-task mean. Our 1.5-point margin on CPU is close to Jev's reported run-to-run spread (up to 1.6).
  • Decido-1-Max and Decido-1-Think write out their reasoning before answering, so they are slow on a laptop (~10–28 s per question) and not bit-identical across batches on a GPU server.
  • Calibration is measured on benchmark tasks. It is not yet measured on agent traces such as tool calls or shell commands; validate thresholds on your own data before automating.
